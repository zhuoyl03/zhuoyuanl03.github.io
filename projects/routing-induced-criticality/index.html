Testing the Transferability of Critical-Satellite Rankings in LEO Networks

Zhuoyuan Li · Manuscript, 2026

Manuscript PDF

Testing the reuse of a failure ranking

Two satellite rankings can have little overlap yet capture similar failure damage. This paper evaluates the cost of reusing a list directly: remove satellites individually, reroute the same flows, and compare the source policy's list with the target policy's own damage-ranked list.

I developed the common policy-dependency universe, paired-flow comparison, and Damage Capture / Transfer Regret evaluation. Reachability, propagation, rerouting, and peak-link-stress damage remain separate outcomes, with explicit handling of ties and zero-damage cases.

What transfers, and what does not

For propagation damage, shortest-distance and hop-count rankings transfer well in the diagnostic experiment. The confirmed transfer loss concerns a different outcome: utilization-aware rankings transfer poorly to IGP-ECMP for peak-link-stress damage.

Across 24 sampled orbital phases, that direction has median top-20 Transfer Regret of 0.248, with a traffic-cluster bootstrap interval of [0.150, 0.333], and meets the six-phase, three-hash confirmation criterion. The corresponding shortest-distance direction does not meet that confirmation.

Testing possible explanations

Two further experiments test whether pre-deletion traffic features predict transfer loss and whether expanding inter-satellite links and ground access mitigates it. Neither meets its locked success criterion. The manuscript retains these results rather than presenting an unverified general mechanism.

These conclusions concern graph-level failure effects under synthetic traffic and uniform capacity. They do not establish operational latency, a universal instability of critical-satellite rankings, or an improvement from deploying utilization-aware routing.
